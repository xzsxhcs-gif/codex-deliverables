Some places are known for what they make. Yongkang is one of them.

This county-level city is administered by Jinhua, in central Zhejiang Province, eastern China.

Here, hills, old neighborhoods, and a busy manufacturing economy exist side by side.

Yongkang's county history reaches back to the Three Kingdoms period, more than seventeen hundred years ago.

Today, Yongkang is often called China's hardware capital.

Its factories produce goods such as power tools, household doors, and kitchenware.

These ordinary objects help connect this city with everyday life far beyond Zhejiang.

But industry is only part of the story.

To understand a city, it helps to look at both its workplaces and the places where people gather.

At Fangyan, steep cliffs and striking rock formations create a dramatic landscape.

This scenic area is known for its Danxia landforms.

Back in town, Xijin Bridge stretches across the Yongkang River.

Its covered walkway and timber structure offer a quieter view of the city and its history.

Across Yongkang's historic villages, ancestral halls preserve family memories and local craftsmanship.

Traditional celebrations, including the Fangyan Temple Fair, keep parts of that shared culture alive.

There is also something to taste: roumaibing, a savory local flatbread with pork and preserved vegetables.

Yongkang is more than a place that makes things.

It is a place where useful objects, living traditions, and distinctive landscapes come together.

That is a different side of Zhejiang worth getting to know.
